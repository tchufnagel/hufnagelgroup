abstract: |
  The performance of concrete under high pressure and strain rate loading is critical to its use in protective structures, but a comprehensive investigation of damage across length scales in these regimes is currently lacking. Here, we investigate damage in concrete samples subjected to high-velocity projectile impacts and study deformation mechanisms across length scales. Impact tests are conducted using a two-stage gas gun and stainless steel (SS) or aluminum (AL) projectiles. We use crater morphology to assess macroscale damage, high-resolution X-ray micro-computed tomography (micro-CT) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to assess microscale damage, and statistical nanoindentation to assess nanoscale damage. At the same impact energy, impact with SS induces a larger conical crater and a cylindrical tunnel, while impact with AL induces a smaller crater with an underdeveloped tunnel. At the microscale, micro-CT and SEM reveal similar crack morphologies in both experiments, with slightly wider crack openings in the sample impacted with SS. Intragranular fracture is only observed in a few sand grains at the walls of the crater and tunnel. At the nanoscale, statistical nanoindentation at various depths and radial distances from the point of impact reveals no degradation of moduli and hardness in sand particles but significant degradation in cement that is not observable from micro-CT and SEM. This work provides a comprehensive damage quantification that can be used to predict residual concrete properties following impact events and to calibrate or validate damage predictions in numerical simulations.
