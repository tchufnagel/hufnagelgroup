title: 'Grain-size distribution characterization from single-shot x-ray diffraction using hierarchical Bayesian multi-emitter analysis'

abstract: |
  The distribution of grain sizes in a polycrystalline material can be determined from a 2D x-ray diffraction pattern by counting and characterizing diffraction spots, so long as the spots are discrete. This approach breaks down if there is substantial overlap of the reflections due to either a large number of grains in the scattering volume or crowding of spots caused by crystallographic texture. As a result, the spot-counting approach to grain size determination is infeasible for many situations of practical interest.

  We address this limitation by applying hierarchical probabilistic learning using a reversible-jump Markov chain Monte Carlo framework to jointly infer diffraction spot populations and their intensity distributions. Using synthetic diffraction patterns, we show how this hierarchical model allows recovery of diffraction peak intensities across a wide variety of grain-size distributions and crystallographic textures. From the recovered peak intensities we infer grain-size distributions themselves and characterize the regimes of grain size and texture for which this approach is practical. Finally, we demonstrate the application of our technique by comparing grain-size distributions in Cu-Ti alloy specimens obtained from 2D x-ray diffraction patterns with those from electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD). This method of grain-size distribution determination is expected to be particularly useful in the context of high-throughput experimentation, where the minimal sample preparation requirements and short exposure times of single-shot x-ray diffraction are substantial advantages relative to microscopy techniques.
